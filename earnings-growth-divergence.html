Methodology

EPS growth compares each company's latest quarterly EPS to the same quarter a year earlier, so seasonal swings (like a retailer's Q4 vs. Q3) don't distort it. Companies that had a loss in the year-ago quarter are left out, since going from a loss to a profit doesn't produce a meaningful growth rate.

Price performance is each stock's 3-month return relative to the S&P 500.

Every stock is ranked twice: once by EPS growth (1 = fastest growing) and once by 3-month relative price return (1 = best performing). The divergence score is the difference between the two ranks. A large positive score means the stock's price has done much better than its earnings growth, so the market is either paying for something other than past results or pricing in growth that hasn't been reported yet. A large negative score means the opposite: earnings are growing faster than the stock is moving. That can be an overlooked opportunity, or the market correctly discounting something the past EPS number doesn't show (rising costs, a slowing outlook, a one-time tax benefit). This page shows the divergence, but doesn't say which explanation applies.
