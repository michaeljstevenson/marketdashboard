Valuations by Industry

Aggregated valuation multiples across ~7,000 U.S. public companies, grouped into 94 industry classifications — price/earnings, EV/EBITDA, price/book, and expected growth.
Annual, updated each January
Total Market shown for reference. Click a column to sort.

Each row aggregates every publicly traded U.S. company Damodaran's dataset covers within that industry classification (~7,000 companies total across 94 industries). Current PE uses trailing 12-month diluted EPS including one-time items; Trailing PE excludes them; Forward PE uses next-12-month analyst EPS estimates. PEG divides the PE ratio by the expected 5-year earnings growth rate — a PEG near 1 is the classic rule-of-thumb for "fairly priced relative to growth," well above 1 suggests growth may be overpriced, below 1 potentially underpriced (though this rule of thumb has real limitations across industries with very different risk and margin profiles). EV/EBITDA compares enterprise value to earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization — useful for comparing capital structures across industries with different debt levels. ROE is aggregate net income divided by aggregate book equity for money-making firms in the industry.

Limitations: industry aggregates can be skewed by a small number of very large or very high-multiple companies within a group — averages here aren't sales-weighted or median-based. Data is annual, refreshed each January, not a live daily feed. A very high PE or PEG in a small, immature, or high-money-losing-share industry often reflects the small denominator effect of aggregating mostly unprofitable early-stage companies, not genuine overvaluation of the profitable ones.

Source: Aswath Damodaran, NYU Stern — Industry-average valuation multiples (PE, EV/EBITDA, Price/Book)
