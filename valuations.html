As of the October 7, 2026 close

The S&P 500 trades at 24.1 times its last four quarters of reported earnings and 20.0 times what analysts expect over the next year. On the rebuilt history since 2017, the trailing multiple sits in the 65th percentile, above its median of 22.1 but well short of the 36.4 reached in March 2021, when trailing earnings still included the 2020 slump.

Technology carries the richest trailing multiple among the large sectors (32.6), but on forward earnings it's 23.0, close to Industrials and Consumer Discretionary, because analysts expect its earnings to keep growing quickly. Energy (17.6 trailing, 12.9 forward) and Financials (15.8 and 14.4) are the cheapest.

Health Care stands out against its own past. At 22.5 times trailing earnings it's in the 90th percentile of its history, while Real Estate is at its lowest reading and Communication Services is in its 18th percentile.

The S&P 500 figures update monthly. Each sector's or industry's multiple is its total market value divided by its total earnings, book value or sales, losses included, which is how index providers compute an index P/E. Trailing P/E uses each company's reported EPS for its last four quarters, the adjusted figure companies and analysts quote, times shares outstanding. Forward P/E uses the forward P/E in Alpha Vantage's company overview, which is based on analysts' consensus EPS estimates. EV/EBITDA, price/book and price/sales use the same overview's enterprise value, EBITDA, book value and revenue, counting each company once across its share classes, and EV/EBITDA leaves out financials.

The history is rebuilt, so it's an approximation. It starts once at least 80% of today's members have four quarters of reported EPS in the data, which is late 2017 for most sectors. It takes today's members and today's share counts and values them at each past month-end with the price and reported EPS known then. Companies that left the index, and today's members before they joined, are counted as if the index were today's list throughout, and buybacks and share issuance since then aren't reflected. It shows how today's index would have been valued, which is close to, but not the same as, the published index P/E at the time. From the first monthly run on, each month's actual reading is also kept.

Each row aggregates every publicly traded US company Damodaran's dataset covers within that industry classification. Current PE uses trailing 12-month diluted EPS including one-time items, trailing PE excludes them, forward PE uses next-12-month analyst EPS estimates. PEG divides the PE ratio by the expected 5-year earnings growth rate. ROE is aggregate net income divided by aggregate book equity for money-making firms in the industry.

Sources: S&P 500 multiples from Alpha Vantage company overviews and earnings and Yahoo Finance prices. Aswath Damodaran, NYU Stern: Industry-average valuation multiples (PE, EV/EBITDA, Price/Book).
