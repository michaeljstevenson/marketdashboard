Methodology

Universe is the full S&P 500. For every constituent, Alpha Vantage's INCOME_STATEMENT and BALANCE_SHEET endpoints (quarterly) are pulled and joined on fiscal quarter-end date. Gross/operating/net margin are gross profit, operating income, and net income each divided by total revenue for that quarter. Leverage is net debt (total debt, preferring Alpha Vantage's combined shortLongTermDebtTotal field, minus cash & equivalents) divided by trailing-twelve-month EBITDA — EBITDA uses Alpha Vantage's direct ebitda field where populated, falling back to operating income plus depreciation & amortization (both on the same income statement) when it isn't. A handful of fields come back as the literal string "None" rather than being omitted on some tickers — those are treated as missing, and a company-quarter is dropped from a given metric rather than silently coerced to zero.

Sector series are the median across each SPDR sector's constituents for that calendar quarter (a fiscal quarter-end date is bucketed into whichever calendar quarter its month falls in — a company with a non-calendar fiscal year, e.g. a January quarter-end, can land one quarter off from its own internal numbering, which doesn't affect a sector median but is worth knowing if a single ticker's quarter label looks unexpected). A sector-quarter median is only published once at least 3 companies report that quarter.

The regime comparison classifies each calendar quarter as Hiking/Holding/Cutting using the identical trailing-3-month effective-Fed-funds-rate-change thresholds (>0.1pp hiking, <-0.1pp cutting, otherwise holding) already used on Small Cap vs. Large Cap Spread. Rather than testing each company's quarterly change separately (~500 pseudo-replicated points per quarter, which would mechanically inflate significance), the test is run on one point per calendar quarter — the cross-company median change in operating margin, and separately in net-debt/EBITDA, regressed against that quarter's Fed-funds change (Pearson and Spearman, matching the two-method check used throughout Factor Analysis). With only a few dozen quarters of history and margins/leverage that move slowly and stickily quarter to quarter, this is a small-sample test — a weak or insignificant result here is a legitimate finding, not a sign the test failed, and is reported as such rather than dressed up.

Leaderboards and the per-company table compare each company to its own sector's latest median rather than the raw cross-sector figure, since margin and leverage norms vary hugely by industry (software vs. retail, utilities vs. banks) — a company can show the widest margin on the page and still be unremarkable next to its sector peers, or vice versa.

Refreshes weekly (Saturday mornings) — fundamentals only change when a company files a new 10-Q/10-K. Sector and company name come from the same weekly metadata sweep that backs Sector Beeswarm, reused here rather than paying for a third full-index pass just for labels.
