Methodology

Reuses the same dataset as Sector Performance and this session's Sector Correlation Matrix — the 11 SPDR sector ETFs' daily adjusted-close history against SPY, already fetched by that page's own background job. No new Alpha Vantage calls are made here.

The rotation graph follows the standard Relative Rotation Graph (RRG) framework: a sector's level (x-axis) is its medium-term relative-strength trend (the 3-month change in its price-relative-to-SPY ratio), and its momentum (y-axis) is the short-term change in that same ratio (1-month). The four quadrants read as: Leading (positive level, positive momentum — outperforming and still accelerating), Weakening (positive level, negative momentum — still outperforming but losing steam), Lagging (negative level, negative momentum — underperforming and still falling behind), Improving (negative level, positive momentum — underperforming but starting to turn). Sectors classically rotate through these quadrants clockwise over a full cycle, though not on any fixed schedule.

The persistence check ranks the 11 sectors by trailing 1-month relative return at each month-end over the available history, then correlates each sector's rank in one month against its rank the following month (Spearman, since ranks are ordinal by construction) across every sector-month pair. A near-zero or negative result would mean chasing last month's sector winners is no better than random — worth knowing before treating any single month's leaderboard as a signal.
