Energy has been the strongest sector over three months, 16.7 points ahead of SPY, but it's already slipped into the weakening quadrant on the rotation graph, as has Technology. Almost every other sector sits in the improving quadrant: still behind SPY, but gaining on it.

Technology is the only sector well ahead over six months (+29.2 points). Everything else has trailed SPY over that stretch, Utilities most of all (-28.5), which says as much about how narrow the market's gains have been as about any one sector.

The rotation model holds Technology, Health Care and Financials for October. Over 26 years it has earned about what SPY did (8.47% a year against 8.24%, and 8.08% after costs), and none of the 15 lookback and holding combinations in the grid shows a reliable edge. Following sector momentum hasn't paid off in a way that's distinguishable from luck.

Methodology

Daily prices for the 11 sector ETFs compared against SPY.

The rotation graph follows the standard Relative Rotation Graph (RRG) setup. The x-axis is each sector's medium-term trend: its RS-Ratio, which is 100 plus the number of standard deviations its relative strength sits above or below its average over the trailing 12 weeks. The y-axis is its momentum: its RS-Momentum, which is 100 plus the same 12-week measure applied to the 1-week percent change in RS-Ratio. The four quadrants are Leading (outperforming and accelerating), Weakening (still outperforming but slowing), Lagging (underperforming and falling further behind) and Improving (underperforming but turning up). Sectors tend to rotate through the quadrants clockwise over a full cycle, though not on any fixed schedule.

Relative strength is 100 times the sector's weekly close divided by SPY's, using the last trading day of each week. The RS-Ratio and RS-Momentum formulas behind Julius de Kempenaer's RRG are proprietary, so the graph here is an approximation of the RRG method. Each tail covers the last 10 weeks. The latest point uses the latest close, so it can move until the week ends. The Quadrant column in the snapshot table uses each sector's latest point on the graph.

The rotation model works from month-end closes. At each month-end it ranks every sector ETF with at least 12 months of history by its trailing return relative to SPY and holds the top three in equal weights for the next month. The signal can be the trailing 3, 6 or 12 months, or 12 months skipping the latest month (12-1), and the default is 6 months. Because every sector is measured against the same SPY return, ranking on relative return gives the same order as ranking on total return. Signals use only closes up to the month-end, and each month's return runs from that close to the next month-end close. The benchmarks are SPY and an equal-weight basket of every sector the model could choose from that month, rebalanced monthly. Returns use split- and dividend-adjusted closes, so they include dividends.

Volatility is the annualized standard deviation of monthly returns. Max drawdown is the largest fall from a peak in daily value. The information ratio is the average monthly return over SPY divided by its standard deviation, annualized. Turnover is the share of the portfolio sold at each monthly rebalance, including trades that reset drifted weights to equal. CAGR after costs deducts 10 basis points per unit of turnover.

The formation and holding grid tests which lookbacks have had predictive value. At each month-end, the same eligible sectors are ranked on their return over the formation period, and a portfolio buys the top three and sells the bottom three in equal weights. Following Jegadeesh and Titman (1993), each month holds equal parts of the portfolios formed at each of the last K month-ends, where K is the holding period, so no monthly return is counted twice. Each cell shows that portfolio's annualized average monthly return and its t-statistic. A t-statistic beyond 2 in either direction is the usual threshold for significance at the 5% level.
