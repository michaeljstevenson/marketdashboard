What these factors are

Market (Mkt−RF) is the broad US stock market's return in excess of the risk-free rate — the baseline "just own the market" premium. Size (SMB, "small minus big") is the return of small-cap stocks minus large-cap stocks. Value (HML, "high minus low") is the return of high book-to-market ("value") stocks minus low book-to-market ("growth") stocks. Momentum is the return of recent winners (top 30% by prior 12-month return, skipping the most recent month) minus recent losers (bottom 30%).

Each line above shows the growth of $1 invested at the start of the selected range in a portfolio that isolates just that factor — e.g. the size line reflects only the small-vs-large spread, not the market's overall direction. A rising SMB line means small caps are outperforming large caps over that stretch; a falling HML line means growth is beating value. These are long-run academic research series (Fama-French, updated by Kenneth French), not investable products — there's no fund that exactly replicates SMB, HML, or momentum as defined here, though many "factor" and "smart beta" funds are built to approximate their spirit.

Daily data begins 1926-07-01 for market/size/value and 1926-11-03 for momentum. Values are Ken French's published daily percent returns (e.g. 0.09 means +0.09% that day); the underlying data updates roughly monthly, so this page's refresh cadence follows suit rather than checking daily.

Source: Ken French Data Library, Tuck School of Business at Dartmouth
