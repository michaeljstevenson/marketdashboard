Methodology

Universe is the full S&P 500. For every constituent, Alpha Vantage's EARNINGS endpoint returns each reported quarter's actual EPS, the analyst consensus estimate on file at the time, and the resulting surprise (both in dollars and as a %) — already computed by Alpha Vantage, not derived here. A quarter only counts if it has a real consensus estimate on file (very early history for some names reports none); each company's most recent 8 qualifying quarters (roughly 2 years) are kept. A quarter is a "beat" if surprise % > 0, a "miss" if < 0, and "inline" if exactly 0.

Sector and market beat rates are pooled at the quarter level, not averaged company-by-company — a company with a full 8 quarters on file contributes 8 data points, one with only 3 contributes 3, so the aggregate reflects actual reporting volume rather than treating every company as equally informative regardless of history depth.

"Beat Persistence" runs a two-proportion z-test on same-company consecutive-quarter pairs (inline quarters dropped from both sides of a pair to keep the comparison a clean beat-vs-miss binary): the beat rate in the quarter immediately following a beat, against the beat rate immediately following a miss. A statistically significant difference would suggest earnings surprises aren't independent draws — that a company beating (or missing) has some tendency to keep doing so, consistent with the "post-earnings-announcement drift" literature this page doesn't otherwise test directly (that would require price data around each report date, out of scope here). Treat the result for what it is: one specific test on one specific dataset, not a trading signal.

Refreshes weekly (Saturday mornings) — a company reports earnings once a quarter, so a trailing-8-quarter history has little reason to move day to day. Sector and company name come from the same weekly metadata sweep that backs Sector Beeswarm, reused here rather than paying for a second full-index pass just for labels.

Source: Alpha Vantage — EARNINGS
