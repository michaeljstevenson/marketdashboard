Methodology

Universe is the full S&P 500. For every constituent, Alpha Vantage's CASH_FLOW endpoint (quarterly reports) supplies the most recent 4 quarters' operatingCashflow and capitalExpenditures. TTM Free Cash Flow is TTM operating cash flow minus TTM capex, computed as OCF − |capex| — the absolute value is deliberate: a live check against Alpha Vantage's data during this page's build showed capitalExpenditures reported as a positive outflow figure (not negative, the sign some other vendors use), and subtracting the absolute value keeps the math correct regardless of which convention a given ticker happens to use. A company is skipped entirely — not partially annualized — if any of its 4 most recent quarterly reports is missing either field.

FCF yield is TTM FCF divided by market cap, as a percentage. Market cap, sector, and company name are not re-fetched here — they're read from the Sector Beeswarm page's own weekly metadata sweep, the same reuse pattern several other pages on this site use rather than paying for a second full-index OVERVIEW pass just for labels. Trailing P/E comes from the Forward vs. Trailing P/E Divergence page's own snapshot, read here read-only; earnings yield is simply 100 ÷ trailing P/E. Because that page's own company list is itself already filtered to tickers with a sane trailing and forward P/E, earnings-yield coverage on this page is a subset of the full FCF-yield universe — a company can have a valid FCF yield with no earnings yield or divergence figure at all, shown as — rather than guessed at. Both of this page's cross-page dependencies are themselves one-time or occasionally-refreshed snapshots as of this writing, not continuously live feeds, so a company newly missing from either can briefly widen that gap further.

A company's FCF yield is dropped entirely (not clamped or capped, just excluded from every chart, table, and average) if its magnitude exceeds ±60% — real S&P 500 constituents essentially never print a genuine trailing FCF yield past that range even in a hard drawdown; a reading beyond it is overwhelmingly a market-cap/FCF unit or metadata-join mismatch, not a real valuation signal, the same kind of sanity bound Forward vs. Trailing P/E Divergence applies to its own P/E ratios (excluded past 300x, not clamped to it).

The history chart is a genuine accumulating time series, not a single snapshot: CASH_FLOW only reports each company's own trailing quarters, not a market-wide series, so each weekly run appends that week's cross-company median FCF yield to a running series rather than replacing it — the same pattern Earnings Revisions and P/E Divergence use for their own trend lines. It starts thin the week this page ships and becomes more informative with every subsequent run.

The cash-conversion scatter regresses each company's FCF yield against its own earnings yield using the same Pearson and Spearman two-method check used throughout Factor Analysis and this site's other regression checks. A company sitting well below the dashed y=x reference line converts a smaller share of its reported earnings into actual cash (more accrual-heavy — larger non-cash charges, working-capital drag, or heavier capex than its income statement alone suggests); a company well above it converts earnings to cash unusually efficiently. A weak or noisy relationship overall is itself informative — it would say GAAP earnings and cash generation are, in practice, not tightly coupled cross-sectionally.

Refreshes on manual runs for now (see this job's own netlify.toml comment) — a TTM free-cash-flow figure only moves when a company files a new 10-Q/10-K, so nothing here needs a daily or even a strict weekly cadence to stay current.

Source: Alpha Vantage — CASH_FLOW, plus market cap/sector via Sector Beeswarm's OVERVIEW-derived metadata and trailing P/E via P/E Divergence's own OVERVIEW sweep
