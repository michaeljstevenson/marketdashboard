Methodology

Free cash flow is operatingCashflow − capitalExpenditures, both read directly off Alpha Vantage's CASH_FLOW quarterly statement. The same endpoint behind Buyback Effectiveness, but reading its capitalExpenditures and netIncome fields instead of the buyback-specific ones that page needed a fallback chain for. Trailing-12-month figures require all four of the most recent quarters to have every field populated; a company with a gap in its most recent filing is left out rather than approximated. Earnings yield here is deliberately computed the same way, trailing net income ÷ market cap, rather than reused from Equity Risk Premium's own trailing-P/E-based figure, so any gap between the two yields on this page reflects a real cash-vs-accrual difference, not two pages' different conventions colliding.

This is a different question from the two valuation-adjacent pages already on this site. Valuations is industry-level (94 classifications) and multiple-based (P/E, EV/EBITDA, P/B), sourced from Damodaran rather than computed. Equity Risk Premium is stock-level but still earnings-based (trailing P/E vs. the 10-year Treasury). Neither asks whether the earnings a P/E ratio is built on actually converted into cash. A company can report a healthy net income while investing so heavily (or recognizing revenue so aggressively) that free cash flow tells a very different story. FCF conversion (free cash flow ÷ net income) is the direct read on that: below 100%, cash generation is lagging reported earnings; above 100%, it's running ahead. Capex-intensive sectors (Utilities, Real Estate, Energy) should structurally run lower conversion than asset-light ones (Information Technology, Communication Services). The sector chart's toggle is there to check that expectation against what the current data actually shows, not to flag every capital-intensive business as a red flag.

The scatter panel runs both Pearson (OLS) and Spearman rank correlation on FCF yield vs. earnings yield across the index, the same two-method check used throughout Factor Analysis. A strong, tight relationship near the 45° line would mean the two yields mostly agree; a weak one, or a cluster of names well below the line, means cash and accrual earnings are telling meaningfully different stories for a chunk of the index.

Sector and company name are joined from the Sector Beeswarm page's own weekly metadata refresh, and market cap for both yield calculations comes from that same source. A one-time snapshot as of the date below, not a recurring refresh. Cash flow statements only change when a company files a new 10-Q/10-K, matching the convention this site has settled into for its other recent additions (see each page's own methodology note for which ones).
