Methodology

Universe is the full S&P 500. Alpha Vantage's COMPANY_OVERVIEW endpoint carries two independent sell-side signals per stock: a breakdown of current analyst recommendations (AnalystRatingStrongBuy, Buy, Hold, Sell, and StrongSell) and a consensus AnalystTargetPrice. This page turns the rating breakdown into a single weighted consensus score on the standard 1–5 scale used by services like Zacks (1 = Strong Buy, 5 = Strong Sell, so lower is more bullish), and compares the target price against 50DayMovingAverage to get an implied upside — target / 50-day average − 1. Stocks with fewer than 5 covering analysts are excluded from the consensus score (a 1-analyst "consensus" isn't one), matching the coverage threshold Analyst Estimate Dispersion uses.

The 50-day moving average stands in for "current price" deliberately, not a live quote: fetching an actual last price for all ~500 names would mean a second full sweep (Alpha Vantage has no current-price field on the OVERVIEW payload itself), doubling this page's Alpha Vantage cost for a gap that's usually small — a 50-day average lags a fast-moving stock by construction, so implied upside here should be read as "roughly where the stock has been trading lately," not today's exact closing print. The methodology note is the same trade-off Forward vs. Trailing P/E Divergence and Equity Risk Premium by Sector make elsewhere on this site: derive a second metric from fields a sweep already returns rather than pay for an additional one.

The two scatter charts test real, distinct questions. The first: do a stock's rating breakdown and its price target actually point the same direction? They're reported independently by Alpha Vantage's underlying data provider, so agreement isn't guaranteed — a negative slope (more bullish ratings pairing with higher implied upside) is the "signals agree" case. The second reuses Relative Strength Leaders/Laggards' own trailing 3-month relative-return figure (no separate price sweep) to ask whether price targets simply extrapolate the recent trend — a positive slope would say analysts lean trend-following, a flat or negative one would say they don't (or are actively working against it, i.e. more contrarian on recent losers). Both a linear (Pearson) and rank-based (Spearman) fit are reported, since either can mislead on its own when a relationship isn't linear.

This is a single-snapshot analysis, not an accumulating one: both statistical tests are cross-sectional (drawn from the same data pull), so — unlike some of this site's other analyst-data pages — there's no cold-start wait for history to build up, and no continuous refresh schedule. Re-run manually when a fresh pull is wanted. Sector and company name come from each stock's own COMPANY_OVERVIEW response (GICS-normalized the same way Sector Beeswarm's own metadata sweep is), not a shared cross-page lookup, since this job already pays for the full sweep. That same sweep also carries each stock's institutional/insider ownership percentage and market cap, used by Institutional & Insider Ownership rather than paying for a second sweep of its own.
