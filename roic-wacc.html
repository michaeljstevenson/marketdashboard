Methodology

ROIC = NOPAT ÷ invested capital. NOPAT (net operating profit after tax) is trailing-12-month operating profit (EBIT) × (1 − effective tax rate). The effective tax rate is income tax ÷ pre-tax income, kept between 0% and 50%, and set to the 21% US statutory rate when a company's own rate is negative, zero or missing (a one-time tax benefit can otherwise produce a nonsensical rate). Invested capital is total debt plus shareholder equity, minus cash.

WACC blends the cost of equity and the cost of debt. Cost of equity uses CAPM: the 10-year Treasury yield plus the company's beta × a market equity risk premium (a company with no beta is given a beta of 1). The premium is Aswath Damodaran's implied ERP, the same figure shown on the Implied Equity Risk Premium page. Cost of debt is interest expense ÷ total debt, after tax. A company with debt but no reported interest expense is given the Treasury yield plus 1.5 percentage points rather than implying its debt is free. The two costs are weighted by the book value of debt and equity (not market value, as a simplification). Spread = ROIC − WACC. A positive spread means the company earns more on its capital than that capital costs.

Companies with zero or negative shareholder equity are left out. Heavy, sustained buybacks (McDonald's, Starbucks and others) can push book equity below zero, which breaks both the invested capital calculation and the WACC weighting.

Two regressions compare companies at one point in time. Spread vs. beta asks whether riskier companies actually earn more above their cost of capital. Spread vs. 3-month relative return asks whether the market is currently rewarding companies that create value. Both use Pearson (linear) and Spearman (rank) correlation.
