Methodology

This page uses the summary totals from institutional 13F filings for each S&P 500 company: the number of institutional holders, how many increased, decreased or held their position, and institutional ownership as a % of shares outstanding.

Two flow measures are shown because they answer different questions. Holder breadth = holders increased ÷ (holders increased + holders decreased) × 100. It's a headcount: of the institutions that changed their position, what share added? Above 50% means more added than trimmed. Net share flow = (shares added − shares sold) ÷ total institutional shares × 100. It measures size, so a few big funds moving a lot of stock show up even if most holders didn't move. A stock can have positive breadth (most holders adding) and negative net flow (the sellers sold a lot more) at the same time, which is why both are shown.

The flow vs. momentum test compares each stock's net share flow with its 3-month return relative to the S&P 500, using Pearson (linear) and Spearman (rank) correlation. It's a comparison across companies at one point in time, not a prediction.

13F filings are quarterly and can come out up to 45 days after quarter-end, so this is a slow, backward-looking picture. They only cover US institutional managers with at least $100M, and only their long positions (no shorts, options, retail or non-US institutions). A rising ownership % can also just come from buybacks shrinking the share count. Leaderboards require at least 5 institutional holders to cut down on noise.

Sector medians are only shown when at least 3 companies in the sector have a value.
