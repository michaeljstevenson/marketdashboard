Methodology

This page is separate from Sentiment Index. That page is one market-wide gauge built from positioning, volatility, breadth and credit indicators. This page scores individual companies and sectors by what recent financial news says about them. Different question, different data.

Sentiment score. The universe is the S&P 500. For each company, Alpha Vantage's NEWS_SENTIMENT endpoint is queried with tickers=<symbol>&limit=50, which returns up to the 50 most recent matched articles. For each article, the ticker_sentiment entry for that company is located directly (an article can mention several companies in any order). Articles with no matching entry are skipped. The weighted sentiment score is the relevance-weighted average of ticker_sentiment_score: each score multiplied by relevance_score, summed, then divided by the sum of relevance_score. Articles where either field fails to parse are skipped.

Article count and labels. Article count is how many articles matched. It measures attention, which is a separate question from tone. Bullish/bearish counts (plus neutral, somewhat-bullish and somewhat-bearish) are a tally of each article's own ticker_sentiment_label, Alpha Vantage's five-bucket scale, and give a second view next to the weighted average. Everything else about each article is discarded once these values are extracted.

A company with fewer than 5 matched articles is tagged "insufficient coverage" in the full table and excluded from the sector chart, both scatter charts, the regression and all three leaderboards. With only a handful of articles, the weighted average is dominated by whichever one or two ran that week. Five is a low floor that blunts a single outlier while still leaving most of the S&P 500 eligible in most weeks. Every company still appears in the full table.

NEWS_SENTIMENT returns only a snapshot: the latest ~50 articles as of each run, with no historical series. Each run's market-wide median weighted-sentiment score is added to a running history, so the trend line is short at first and only becomes meaningful after several weeks of runs.

Forward-return test. The regression uses the 3-month price return relative to SPY from Relative Strength Leaders/Laggards. If that hasn't refreshed, everything else still shows and a banner says the regression is unavailable.

Sector and company name come from Sector Beeswarm. Data is refreshed manually.

Source: Alpha Vantage: NEWS_SENTIMENT and Relative Strength Leaders/Laggards data.
