Why breadth indicators disagree

Breadth indicators answer two different questions. The A-D line, the % above the 200-day moving average and new highs vs. new lows measure participation: how many stocks are rising, holding an uptrend or making new highs. They move with the market itself, so all three look strong in a broad rally and weak in a sell-off.

The % of members beating the index measures relative breadth. It compares every stock to the cap-weighted S&P 500, so it asks whether the typical stock is keeping up with the largest ones. It barely moves with the direction of the market. It runs high when the biggest stocks lag, as in 2000 to 2002 and in 2022, and low when a few mega-caps carry the index, as in 1998 to 1999 and 2023 to 2024.

The two can point in opposite directions at the same time. On March 1, 2024, 76% of members were above their 200-day average, yet only 23% had beaten the index over the prior year. On October 12, 2022, only 17% were above their 200-day average, while 52% had held up better than the index. Reading them together separates a broad advance from one carried by a narrow group of leaders.

An index can continue climbing while less and less of its constituents are actually going climbing with it. A handful of mega-caps can carry the index even as the typical stock lags or falls. The advance-decline line tracks the running tally of advancing minus declining names, day by day. Its shape relative to the index itself is the signal. Breadth divergence is when the market makes a new high but the A-D line doesn't confirm it. This has historically been a warning sign that the participation is narrowing and sometimes points to a future broader pullback.

The % above the 200-day moving average: How many constituents are in a durable uptrend (instead of just up on a given day). A market near all-time highs with a low reading % above the 200-day SMA means the advance is being carried by a narrow group of leaders rather than broad participation. New highs vs. new lows: a rising count of fresh 52-week lows even while the index holds up is an early-warning breadth signal.

Breadth can diverge from price for months before it matters.  A-D, % above 200-day SMA, 52-week highs vs. lows are simply context for how healthy an advance or decline is underneath the headline index number.
