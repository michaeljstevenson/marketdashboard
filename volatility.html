The market is relatively calm by its own recent standards. The VIX closed at 15.0, the 32nd percentile since 1990, only the 11th percentile of the past year. The S&P 500's realized volatility over 20 days was 10.4%.

As is usually the case, options are priced for more volatility than the market has been delivering. The VIX sits 4.6 points above trailing realized volatility, close to the typical premium. Measured against the volatility that actually followed, the VIX was higher on 85% of days since 1990, by a median of 4.7 points. The exceptions cluster in sudden selloffs, when realized volatility spikes past what was priced.

The VIX term structure remains in contango, upward sloping (12.0 for nine days, 15.0 for one month and 17.6 for three months, with VIX at 0.85 of VIX3M). It's been inverted on only 11% of days since 2006, mostly in selloffs.

Counts are trading days. The return windows overlap, so neighboring days share most of the same return and a count overstates the number of independent observations.

Realized volatility measures how much the S&P 500 has actually moved over the trailing 20 trading days (the standard deviation of daily log returns, annualized). Implied volatility (VIX) measures what options are pricing in for the next 30 days, derived from the premiums that options traders are willing to pay.

Realized volatility is calculated as σ = √( Σ (rₜ − r̄)² / 19 ) × √252 × 100, where rₜ = ln(Pₜ / Pₜ₋₁) is the daily log return of the S&P 500 close over each of the last 20 trading days and r̄ is the average of those 20 returns.

Implied and realized volatility usually move together, but the gap between them (volatility risk premium, VRP) is rarely zero. Implied volatility tends to sit above realized. Option sellers demand compensation for the risk they're taking on, so options are on average priced relatively higher than what volatility actually turns out to be. A widening VRP can be due to rising hedging demand or anticipation of an upcoming event (earnings, Fed meetings, etc.). A falling or negative VRP (implied vol trading below realized) is unusual (typically after a volatility spike).
