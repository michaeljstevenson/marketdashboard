Methodology

Universe is the full S&P 500. For every constituent plus SPY, Alpha Vantage's TIME_SERIES_DAILY_ADJUSTED endpoint (compact — the trailing ~100 trading days) provides split/dividend-adjusted daily closes. Trailing return is measured from the close 21 trading days ago (1-month) and 63 trading days ago (3-month) to the latest close. Relative — or "excess" — return is computed as (1 + stock return) / (1 + SPY return) − 1 rather than a simple subtraction, so it compounds consistently at larger magnitudes; the same construction used on Small Cap vs. Large Cap Spread and the International vs. US page.

A stock is tagged "Leader" if its 3-month relative return ranks in the top fifth of the universe that week, "Laggard" if it ranks in the bottom fifth, and "Neutral" otherwise. This deliberately uses a 3-month, not 1-month, lookback for ranking — 1-month figures are shown for context but are noisier and more prone to single-event whipsaws (an earnings beat, a guidance cut).

The persistence chart above tests whether this week's relative-strength ranking says anything about what happens next — but comparing one week's 3-month rank against next week's 3-month rank would be a trap: two 63-trading-day windows one week apart share 58 of those days, so their ranks would correlate almost mechanically regardless of any real momentum effect (the same overlapping-window pitfall called out in Share Count Trends' own methodology). Instead, each point pairs a stock's rank as of one weekly snapshot against its realized relative return over the following snapshot interval — a forward window built from entirely new trading days, sharing nothing with the rank that predicts it. Pooled across every stock and every consecutive pair of snapshots retained (up to 20 weekly snapshots, roughly five months), a positive, statistically significant Pearson/Spearman relationship would mean today's leaders tend to keep outperforming going forward; a null or negative one would say the opposite — that relative strength mean-reverts, or says nothing about the future at this horizon. As with every regression on this site, both a linear (Pearson) and rank-based (Spearman) fit are reported since one can be misleading when the relationship is nonlinear or driven by outliers.

Refreshes weekly (Saturday, after the rest of that morning's data jobs) — a 21/63-trading-day momentum read barely moves day to day, and the persistence test needs its weekly snapshots spaced apart to mean anything. Sector and company name come from the same weekly metadata sweep that backs Sector Beeswarm, reused here rather than paying for a second full-index pass just for labels. Because the persistence test is built from accumulated weekly snapshots rather than a single run's data, it will show "not enough history yet" for the first week or two after this page goes live and fill in from there.
