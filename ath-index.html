How to read this

This tracks the same S&P 500 constituent list used elsewhere on this site for market breadth internals (advance/decline line, new highs/lows, % above 200-day SMA). The list itself is a periodically-refreshed static snapshot rather than a live index feed, so it can drift slightly out of date on additions, removals, or ticker changes — see breadth-constituents.js for details.

A name counts as being at an all-time high when its most recent close is at or above every prior close in Alpha Vantage's available daily history for that symbol — which for most of these long-listed, liquid names reaches back to the 1990s, but isn't guaranteed to extend all the way to a company's actual IPO in every case. Prices are split- and dividend-adjusted, so a historical stock split or dividend payment doesn't create a false ceiling a stock can never climb back above.

A high reading means strength is broad — many names participating, not just a handful of mega-caps carrying the market. A reading near zero doesn't necessarily mean a bear market; it can simply mean the market has pulled back from a recent peak across the board, which is normal even in an uptrend.
