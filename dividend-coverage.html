"Earnings Payout" and "FCF Payout" are blank where TTM net income or TTM free cash flow was zero/negative (shown as "neg." in the table) — a payout ratio isn't meaningful against a non-positive base, and a negative base is itself a stronger risk signal than any ratio number would be. "Gap" is FCF Payout minus Earnings Payout, in percentage points.

Methodology

Two payout ratios, same TTM window. Both come from Alpha Vantage's CASH_FLOW statement, trailing 12 months (last 4 reported quarters). The dividend figure is dividendPayoutCommonStock (falling back to the combined dividendPayout field on the rare filer where the common-stock-specific one is missing). The earnings payout ratio divides that by TTM netIncome — the "accounting" view, the one usually quoted. The FCF payout ratio divides it by TTM free cash flow (operatingCashflow − capitalExpenditures) — whether the company actually generated enough spare cash to cover what it paid out, independent of non-cash accounting items. Both use the exact same numerator, so any difference between them is a real cash-vs-accrual gap, not a methodology mismatch.

How this differs from the site's other dividend/buyback pages. Dividend Growth Screener tracks whether the dividend has grown (streak, CAGR) — nothing about whether it's safe. Shareholder Yield combines dividend and buyback yield into one magnitude number. Buyback Effectiveness asks a completely different capital-allocation question about repurchases, not dividends. None of them test dividend sustainability — this page applies the same reported-earnings-vs-real-cash lens as Earnings Quality (Accruals), but specifically to the question "can they keep paying this."

Tests. The earnings-vs-FCF scatter is a self-contained cross-sectional Pearson+Spearman regression (same two-method check as Factor Analysis) of the two ratios against each other — how tightly the accounting view and the cash view agree. The FCF-payout-vs-return scatter reuses Relative Strength Leaders/Laggards' own weekly snapshot for each payer's 3-month return relative to SPY, testing whether a stretched payout ratio already shows up as underperformance. Both are single-snapshot, contemporaneous cross-sections — not a lagged or forward-return replication of any published dividend-cut-prediction study — and are described as such rather than implied to be predictive.

Companies with zero reported dividend payout across all 4 trailing quarters are treated as non-payers and excluded entirely, matching Dividend Growth Screener's convention of only screening established payers. Sector and company name come from Sector Beeswarm. This page is a one-time snapshot as of the date below, not a recurring refresh, since quarterly cash flow statements don't move day to day.
