Methodology

Every trading day's return has two parts. The overnight part runs from yesterday's close to today's open, which is when the market is closed and prices move on news, futures trading and the opening auction. The intraday part runs from today's open to today's close. Chained together they equal the ordinary daily return, so the two legs are a clean split of the same total.

The split uses Alpha Vantage's daily TIME_SERIES_DAILY_ADJUSTED history, which carries open, close and a dividend- and split-adjusted close. Each day's raw open is rescaled by that day's adjusted-to-raw close ratio and compared with the prior day's adjusted close. Without that step every ex-dividend morning would look like a small overnight loss. Any day where either leg moved more than 20% is dropped as a bad print, and the count dropped is kept in the data. Returns are log returns; the annualized figures compound the daily returns over 252 trading days.

The headline test is a paired t-test on the daily difference between the overnight and intraday return, run on the full sample, on each decade, and on the first versus second half of the sample, so one unusual stretch can't carry the result. Its p-values treat daily returns as independent, which is a fair approximation for index returns but not exact. The gap-fade check sorts days by overnight return and looks at what the market did next that same day, then runs a Pearson (linear) and Spearman (rank) regression of intraday on overnight return, the same two-method check used on Factor Analysis.

A gap between the legs is a statistical description, not a trading strategy. Capturing only the overnight leg means buying every close and selling every open, which would face real-world trading costs and, for most investors, no practical way to hold at the exact auction prices. See Lou, Polk and Skouras (2019), "A Tug of War: Overnight Versus Intraday Expected Returns," Journal of Financial Economics, for the academic treatment. Not investment advice.

Source: Alpha Vantage TIME_SERIES_DAILY_ADJUSTED (full daily history for SPY, QQQ and IWM), refreshed each weekday after the close.
