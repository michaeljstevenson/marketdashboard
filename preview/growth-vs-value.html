Methodology

Growth and value are represented by two ETFs: IWF (Russell 1000 Growth) and IWD (Russell 1000 Value), with SPY (S&P 500) as the blended benchmark in the return table. Returns include dividends. Both funds launched in May 2000, which is where the ratio starts at 100. Prices come from Alpha Vantage daily adjusted data, and the 10-year Treasury yield comes from Alpha Vantage's monthly Treasury series.

The idea being tested is that growth stocks behave like long-duration assets, since more of their value sits in earnings far in the future, so a rising yield should hurt them more than value stocks. Each month's growth-minus-value return is regressed on that month's change in the 10-year yield. The slope is the "rate beta": how many percentage points of monthly spread return come with a 1-point rise in the yield.

Because the relationship may not hold steady, the page checks it three ways: the rolling 36-month slope over time, the full sample split into an earlier and a later half (the table under the scatter), and Pearson (linear) plus Spearman (rank) correlation so a few extreme months can't drive the result. Months are also grouped by the trailing 3-month yield trend. Monthly data gives only a few hundred observations in total and a few dozen in each yield-trend group, so treat the results as suggestive, not conclusive. Not investment advice.
