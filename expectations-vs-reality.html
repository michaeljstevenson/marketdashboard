Every year by early January, major banks and independent strategists publish where they expect the S&P 500 to finish that calendar year. I compiled these into a consensus figure, an average or median across roughly 10-20 named strategists, plotted here as the "target." 

The pattern that shows up consistently is that for almost every year, strategists cluster their targets around a "normal" high-single-digit gain, while actual returns are far more volatile. Both to the upside (2019, 2021, 2023, 2024, all beat consensus by wide margins) and the downside (2018, 2022). 

Sources: BusinessWeek (via Seeking Alpha archive), CNNMoney/Yahoo Finance strategist roundups, Bloomberg surveys (via CNBC, Financial Samurai, globalmarketsinvestor.substack.com compilations, and 6meridian.com's Bloomberg-sourced retrospective chart), Barron's Big Money Poll. Actual year-end closes from public S&P 500 index data. Individual year sourcing varies since no single publisher has run a consistent annual survey across the full period — see the Source column in the table above.
