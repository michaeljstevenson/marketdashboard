Methodology

Data. Report dates, timing and consensus EPS estimates come from Alpha Vantage's earnings calendar (next three months); each company's reported-EPS history and past surprises come from Alpha Vantage's quarterly earnings endpoint. Sector, name and market cap come from the Sector Beeswarm page's weekly metadata. Only S&P 500 constituents reporting within the next 45 days are shown, and the page refreshes weekly.

Implied growth is the consensus estimate for the upcoming quarter compared with the EPS the company actually reported for the same fiscal quarter a year earlier. It is the growth the Street is asking for. Estimates or year-ago figures under 5¢ are dropped, and so are growth rates beyond ±200%, because dividing by a near-zero number produces meaningless percentages. Companies missing either input show a dash.

The bar-setting test. Using every company-quarter in the history of the companies on this page, we compute the growth the consensus implied then, and compare it with the surprise that followed. We report Pearson and Spearman correlations (the second is less sensitive to outliers) and a five-group breakdown. The idea being tested is that companies expected to grow fast face a harder comparison and are more likely to disappoint. A correlation near zero means the bar height has told us little about the size of the surprise.

Limits. Quarters from the same season share market-wide conditions, so the observations are not fully independent and the p-values are best read as descriptive. Only companies reporting soon are in the sample, and the roughly two-year window of surprises reflects the endpoint's history. Trailing beat rates are backward-looking and are not a forecast. Nothing here is investment advice.
