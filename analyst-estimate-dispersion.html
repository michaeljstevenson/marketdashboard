Methodology

For every S&P 500 constituent, Alpha Vantage's EARNINGS_ESTIMATES endpoint returns not just the consensus (average) EPS estimate for the current fiscal year, but the high and low estimates too, along with how many analysts contribute. Dispersion here is a relative range: (High − Low) ÷ |Average| × 100 — a simple, scale-free way to compare disagreement across stocks with very different EPS levels. It's not a standard deviation (Alpha Vantage doesn't expose the full per-analyst distribution, only the high/low/average/count), so treat it as a reasonable proxy for spread, not a precise one. Leaderboards and the scatter panel require at least 5 covering analysts — with fewer, a wide high/low spread is usually just one outlier estimate, not real disagreement.

This is a different question from what Earnings Revisions tracks on this site. That page asks whether the consensus itself is moving, and how fast (Net Revision Ratio and Estimate Drift). This page asks how much analysts disagree with each other at a single point in time — a stock can have a tightly-clustered consensus that's moving fast, or a widely-split one that's stable. The scatter panel is a first attempt at connecting the two: whether stocks with more analyst disagreement also see more analysts actually changing their estimate (in either direction) over the trailing 30 days, i.e. whether disagreement coincides with more back-and-forth, not just a wider gap that sits still.

The scatter panel runs the same two-method check used throughout Factor Analysis: Pearson (OLS) correlation alongside Spearman rank correlation, so a relationship can't be quietly resting on one or two extreme stocks. Sector and company name are joined from the Sector Beeswarm page's own weekly metadata refresh rather than a second per-ticker lookup.

Refreshes weekly (Saturday), after the site's other full-index sweeps — a current-fiscal-year estimate's high/low spread doesn't meaningfully change day to day, and a 503-ticker sweep is the kind of job this site reserves for weekly cadence. The history chart above starts accumulating from this page's first run and is not backfilled, since Alpha Vantage's revision-count fields are trailing windows, not a queryable history.
