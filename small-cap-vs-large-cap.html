Methodology

Small-cap and large-cap exposure are proxied by two liquid, dividend-adjusted ETFs rather than the underlying indices themselves: IWM (iShares Russell 2000 ETF) for small-caps and SPY (SPDR S&P 500 ETF Trust) for large-caps, with MDY (SPDR S&P MidCap 400 ETF Trust) added as a mid-cap rung for the return ladder below. Alpha Vantage's TIME_SERIES_DAILY_ADJUSTED adjusted-close series (dividends and splits applied) gives a true total-return comparison for all three. The ratio series is indexed to 100 at IWM's May 2000 inception — the binding constraint on how far back this page can go, even though SPY and MDY both trade back to the 1990s.

The Fed-regime split classifies each calendar month by the trailing 3-month change in the effective federal funds rate (Alpha Vantage FEDERAL_FUNDS_RATE): a rise of more than 0.10 percentage points is "Hiking," a fall of more than 0.10pp is "Cutting," and anything in between is "Holding." The intuition being tested is that small-caps, which on average carry more floating-rate debt and less pricing power than large-caps, should be more sensitive to the direction and speed of rate changes — but note the sample sizes are modest (a few dozen months per regime across just one rate cycle's worth of history since 2000), so treat the regime averages as suggestive, not a robust statistical result.

The scatter panel runs the same two-method check used throughout Factor Analysis: Pearson (OLS) correlation alongside Spearman rank correlation, so a relationship can't be quietly resting on one or two extreme months. Both are shown; a low r² here isn't a bug — a single macro variable rarely explains much of a monthly return series, and the point of showing the regression is to be honest about how weak (or strong) that single-variable relationship actually is, not to oversell it.

Refreshes once daily after the US market close (index levels move every trading day, unlike the site's sparse-event or weekly-refresh datasets); the Fed funds rate and Treasury yield series are re-pulled in full each run since both are cheap, low-volume monthly series.
