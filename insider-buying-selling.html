Methodology

Universe is the full S&P 500. For every constituent, Alpha Vantage's INSIDER_TRANSACTIONS endpoint returns each Form 4 line item filed by officers, directors, and 10%+ owners — company, filer, security type, transaction date, shares, price, and whether it was an acquisition or disposal. Only transactions dated within the trailing 90 days as of the last refresh are counted.

Raw Form 4 data is dominated by routine equity-compensation mechanics, not discretionary trading decisions: RSUs vesting, options being exercised, and the automatic tax-withholding sale that immediately follows a vesting event. None of that reflects an insider choosing to buy or sell. This page keeps only security_type = Common Stock transactions reporting a real, positive share price — RSU vesting and stock awards report a blank or zero price, so this filters most of that noise out. It is an honest partial fix, not a complete one: a pre-scheduled 10b5-1 plan sale (an executive who set up automatic periodic sales months in advance, unrelated to any current view on the stock) still reports a normal share price and passes this filter indistinguishably from a discretionary open-market sale. Treat the sell side of this data with real skepticism for that reason.

Insider buying doesn't have the same problem — there's no routine, automatic mechanism that makes an executive spend their own cash to buy more stock, which is why academic research on this topic (e.g. Lakonishok & Lee, 2001, Are Insider Trades Informative?) consistently finds insider purchases carry more signal than insider sales. This page reflects that asymmetry directly: cluster buying (2+ distinct insiders buying with zero sellers in the window) is surfaced as the headline signal, and the largest individual purchases get their own table, while the largest sales are shown only for completeness with an explicit caveat rather than framed as bearish.

Sector and company name come from the same weekly metadata Alpha Vantage sweep that backs Sector Beeswarm, reused here rather than paying for a second full-index pass just for labels. Refreshes weekly (Saturday mornings) — a single company's insider filings are sparse enough that a trailing-90-day aggregate has little reason to move day to day.
