Methodology

A different question from this site's other buyback pages. Share Count Trends, Shareholder Yield and Buyback Effectiveness all ask whether a company bought back stock and how much. This page doesn't re-ask that — it asks where in its own trading range the stock was every time the company actually pulled the trigger, and what the stock did afterward. A company that buys back a lot of stock, timed consistently near its own highs, is a different story than one that buys back the same amount near its own lows.

Buyback spend comes from Alpha Vantage's CASH_FLOW statement, using the same proceedsFromRepurchaseOfEquity sign-convention Buyback Effectiveness uses (negative = cash spent on repurchases that quarter; a few tickers report through the differently-named paymentsForRepurchaseOfCommonStock/paymentsForRepurchaseOfEquity fields instead, used as a fallback). Only quarters with a real, non-zero repurchase count as a "buyback event" here.

Range percentile is where that day's price fell between the stock's own trailing 52-week (252-session) low and high — 0% at the low, 100% at the high — using daily adjusted closes from Yahoo Finance as of the trading day on or before that quarter's fiscal period end. Forward excess return compounds the stock's own return over the year following that day against SPY's return over the same stretch, the same construction used on Small Cap vs. Large Cap Spread and Relative Strength Leaders/Laggards, and is left blank for any quarter less than a year in the past. A company's own figures are dollar-weighted averages across its qualifying quarters (spend that quarter as the weight), so a company that spent five times as much in one quarter as another has that quarter count five times as much toward its average.

Why Yahoo Finance for price history, not Alpha Vantage. This page needs several years of daily closes per company — a 52-week range as of each historical quarter, plus a full year of data after it. Alpha Vantage's daily-adjusted endpoint has no partial-range option (only a ~100-day "compact" or the entire multi-decade "full" history), and Relative Strength Leaders/Laggards' own methodology note already found that pulling "full" across the whole S&P 500 is disproportionate even for a much shorter lookback than this page needs. Yahoo's chart endpoint returns exactly the multi-year window this page actually uses, free of Alpha Vantage's rate limit — the same tradeoff this codebase already made for its other full-universe daily-price sweeps (see Seasonality, Short Sale Volume, Relative Strength Leaders/Laggards).

Tests. The headline test is the scatter: one point per company (its own dollar-weighted average range percentile vs. its own dollar-weighted average forward excess return), so no company's repeated quarters are pseudo-replicated into a false sense of significance — Pearson (OLS) and Spearman rank correlation, same convention as Factor Analysis. The tercile bar chart pools every individual buyback quarter (not one row per company) to show what typically happened next after a purchase near a low, the middle of the range, or a high; because the same company's quarters can land in more than one tercile, it's reported as a descriptive comparison rather than an independent-sample test, with a Welch's t-test (unequal variance, comparing the low and high thirds) reported alongside it for a sense of scale — not as the page's primary evidence, which is the scatter.

Sector and company name come from Sector Beeswarm. This page is a one-time snapshot as of the date below, not a recurring refresh.
